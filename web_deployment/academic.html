Academic Profile

My academic journey began in Mechanical Engineering and is now developing toward Computer Science, with growing interests in computer networks, distributed systems, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, information retrieval, and decentralized computing.

My current objective is to strengthen both the theoretical foundations and practical skills required for advanced study and research in Computer Science . Alongside structured academic preparation, I use practical projects and experiments to develop a deeper understanding of computing systems.

Formal Education

Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech) — Mechanical Engineering

Undergraduate Engineering Degree

My undergraduate education provided a foundation in engineering mathematics, analytical problem solving, technical reasoning, physical systems, and structured engineering approaches.

During and following my engineering education, my interests increasingly expanded toward software, computing systems, programming, networking, and information technology.

Transition to Computer Science

My transition from Mechanical Engineering toward Computer Science has been driven by sustained practical exploration of software, operating systems, computer hardware, networking, servers, automation, and programming.

This practical exposure gradually developed into a broader academic interest in understanding the principles underlying computation, software systems, networks, distributed computing, security, and artificial intelligence.

Current academic direction: Building a stronger Computer Science foundation while developing research-oriented knowledge in networks, distributed systems, cybersecurity, information retrieval, artificial intelligence, cloud/edge computing, and decentralized systems.
