About Me

I am Akshay R. Ijalkar, an engineering graduate developing my academic and technical foundation in Computer Science with a long-term interest in advanced study, research, and the development of practical computing systems.

My academic background began in Mechanical Engineering. Over time, my interests increasingly moved toward computing, particularly the way software, networks, operating systems, distributed infrastructure, security, and intelligent systems work together.

I am currently strengthening my foundations in Computer Science while developing practical experience through programming, systems administration, networking, automation, software projects, server infrastructure, and research-oriented experimentation.

Rather than treating these areas independently, I am particularly interested in understanding how they interact to create useful, resilient, secure, and intelligent computing systems.

What Interests Me

One question that particularly interests me is how computing systems can continue providing useful communication and access to information when conventional Internet infrastructure is limited, unreliable, unavailable, or unsuitable for a particular environment.

This has led me toward exploring peer-to-peer networking, decentralized systems, distributed information retrieval, edge computing, security, and privacy-preserving architectures.

I am also interested in how artificial intelligence can interact with these systems — for example, by assisting information discovery, ranking, routing, resource selection, automation, and decision-making across distributed environments.

These interests currently form the foundation for the research questions and experimental projects documented throughout this portfolio.

Research Direction

My current research exploration investigates decentralized information discovery and retrieval across nearby participating computing devices.

The broader question is whether distributed and peer-to-peer computing architectures can provide useful information services without depending entirely on conventional centralized Internet infrastructure, while maintaining appropriate privacy and security.

This work is currently at an early research and prototype stage. My objective is to progressively develop it through implementation, controlled experiments, quantitative evaluation, analysis, and eventually scholarly communication where the results justify it.
